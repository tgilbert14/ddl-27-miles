# 27 Miles to Canada — Grounding Bible

> Single source of truth for the Mount Lemmon / Catalina Highway scrollytelling build. Every number here is traceable to five research briefs (life zones, road profile, climate, species cast, scroll technique). No em dashes. En dashes only in numeric ranges.

---

## 1. The Spine

Driving the Catalina Highway up the south face of the Santa Catalina Mountains climbs roughly 6,000-6,400 ft in about 27 miles, crossing six biotic communities in the same physiognomic sequence (desert to grassland to oak woodland to pine to fir) that a continental drive from the Sonoran Desert of Mexico to the forests of Canada would cross. **The honest headline we CAN make:** "In 27 miles you climb through the same sequence of life zones you would otherwise drive to the Canadian border to see, because the mountain cools about 25 F from base to summit." **The claim we must NOT make:** that the summit *is* Canada, has Canada's climate, or holds Canadian species. Mount Lemmon tops out in Douglas-fir / white-fir mixed conifer (Merriam's Canadian *zone*), not in true spruce-fir subalpine forest, and it keeps Tucson's photoperiod, sun angle, and bimodal monsoon rainfall. The line is an analogy about community composition and temperature regime, never an identity claim.

## 2. The Bands

Master content spine, base to summit. Elevation bands are interpretive (Palisades Visitor Center / Tucson Community Guide six-zone framing), not sharp lines; real boundaries shift hundreds of feet with slope aspect and have crept upslope since the mid-20th century (Brusca et al. 2013). Mileposts are approximate.

### Band 1 — Sonoran Desertscrub
- **Elevation:** ~2,800-4,000 ft (Merriam Lower Sonoran)
- **Begins:** ~mile 0 (Tanque Verde Rd base)
- **Signature plants:** saguaro, foothill palo verde, ocotillo, prickly pear / cholla, creosote bush
- **Signature animals:** Gila monster, cactus wren, Gambel's quail, javelina, western diamondback rattlesnake
- **Visitor feels:** Heat shimmer and open sky; the saguaro forest you start inside.

### Band 2 — Desert / Semidesert Grassland
- **Elevation:** ~4,000-5,000 ft (lower Upper Sonoran); narrow transitional band
- **Begins:** ~mile 5-6 (Molino Basin, 4,370 ft)
- **Signature plants:** sotol (desert spoon), beargrass, agave (shindagger / Palmer), soaptree yucca, grama grasses
- **Signature animals:** scaled quail, rufous-winged sparrow, gray fox, Sonoran whipsnake, Clark's spiny lizard
- **Visitor feels:** The cactus thins to waving grass and yucca; the first sense of leaving the desert floor.

### Band 3 — Madrean Evergreen Oak Woodland (Encinal) + Chaparral
- **Elevation:** ~5,000-6,000 ft (Upper Sonoran)
- **Begins:** ~mile 10-12
- **Signature plants:** Emory oak, Arizona white oak, Mexican blue oak, alligator juniper, pointleaf manzanita
- **Signature animals:** Mexican jay, acorn woodpecker, Arizona gray squirrel (native), white-nosed coati, Yarrow's spiny lizard
- **Visitor feels:** Shade returns; the "sky island" oak belt shared with the Sierra Madre.

### Band 4 — Pine-Oak Woodland
- **Elevation:** ~6,000-7,000 ft (lower Transition)
- **Begins:** ~mile 13-14 (Windy Point Vista, ~6,400 ft, MP ~14)
- **Signature plants:** Chihuahua pine, Arizona pine, silverleaf oak, Arizona madrone, New Mexico locust
- **Signature animals:** hepatic tanager, Grace's warbler, painted redstart, black bear (ranges through), whiskered screech-owl
- **Visitor feels:** Granite hoodoos and the first tall pines mixing into the oaks; cooler air at the overlook.

### Band 5 — Ponderosa Pine Forest
- **Elevation:** ~7,000-8,000 ft (Merriam Transition)
- **Begins:** ~mile 17 (Rose Canyon, ~7,000 ft)
- **Signature plants:** ponderosa pine (dominant), southwestern white pine, Gambel oak, alligator juniper, bracken fern
- **Signature animals:** Abert's squirrel (tassel-eared, introduced but conspicuous), Steller's jay, pygmy nuthatch, wild turkey, Mexican spotted owl
- **Visitor feels:** Vanilla-bark pine forest and real shade; you could be in the Mogollon Rim country, not the desert you left 40 minutes ago.

### Band 6 — Mixed-Conifer Forest (Summit)
- **Elevation:** ~8,000-9,157 ft (Merriam Canadian zone)
- **Begins:** ~mile 19.6 (Palisades / General Hitchcock Visitor Center, ~7,800-7,920 ft) up through the summit
- **Signature plants:** Douglas-fir, white fir, southwestern white pine, quaking aspen, Rocky Mountain / bigtooth maple
- **Signature animals:** yellow-eyed junco (nests near summit), red-faced warbler, olive warbler, mountain chickadee, greater short-horned lizard
- **Visitor feels:** Aspen, fir shade, and thin cool air; the "Canada" payoff, honestly Douglas-fir and white fir, not spruce.

## 3. The Road

- **Official name:** General Hitchcock Highway (Forest Highway 39); scenic designation Sky Island Parkway National Scenic Byway; commonly the Catalina Highway. Ski Run Road is the final spur from Summerhaven to Ski Valley / observatory.
- **Base elevation:** ~2,700 ft (paved start at Tanque Verde Rd; cyclist standard start at Snyder Rd is 2,760 ft).
- **Summit elevation:** 9,157 ft (most-cited; variants 9,159 ft and 9,171 ft NAVD88). Road terminus at the SkyCenter/observatory is ~9,134 ft, just below the true summit and behind an often-locked gate.
- **Honest total length (state the endpoint every time):** the signed "27 miles" is the rounded byway figure from Tanque Verde Rd to the top-of-highway near Summerhaven / Ski Valley. To Summerhaven village is ~25-26 mi; to the observatory near the summit is ~28-29 mi (PJAMM 28.8 mi). Net gain "over 6,000 ft"; **total climbing ~7,000 ft** once the Summerhaven dip is counted (PJAMM 7,028 ft).
- **CRITICAL — the profile is NOT monotonic.** The road peaks near ~8,000 ft around Palisades (~MP 20-21), DESCENDS ~200+ ft into Summerhaven (7,790 ft, ~MP 24-25), then re-climbs steeply (~7% grade) up Ski Run Road to ~9,150 ft. The drawn climb curve MUST show this dip or it misrepresents the road and understates total climbing.

## 4. The Climate Readout

- **Defensible summit-cooler number:** the summit runs **20-30 F cooler** than the Tucson floor; use **"about 25 F cooler on average"** as the single defensible figure. (Sanity check: ~6.5 thousand-ft units x 3.5 F/1,000 ft = ~23 F, inside the observed 20-30 F spread.) In summer this is ~100-105 F desert highs vs summit highs rarely above ~80 F. Do not state a flat "exactly 30 F."
- **Lapse rate to animate the gauge:** **~3.5 F per 1,000 ft** (environmental / ICAO standard 6.5 C/km). Frame it as a typical average, not a constant; the real daily value ranges between the moist adiabatic (~2.7 F/1,000 ft near surface) and dry adiabatic (~5.4 F/1,000 ft) bounds, and inversions can locally reverse it.
- **Precipitation gradient:** ~12 in/yr on the Tucson floor vs ~28-30 in/yr up top (Summerhaven 29.56 in/yr) — precip roughly doubles to triples with height. Snow: summit averages ~60-65 in/yr (Summerhaven 64.9 in); the desert floor essentially never accumulates snow. About half the annual total falls in the July-September monsoon.
- **Elevation-to-latitude heuristic (present as a hedged range, never a hard number):** commonly cited at ~170 mi north per 1,000 ft (Hopkins' law), ~200 mi (Merriam), up to ~300 mi (popular). Recommended convention to quote: **~200-300 miles north per 1,000 ft**. Applied to the ~6,500 ft climb this lands "somewhere between the northern US and central Canada" (roughly 1,300-1,900 mi north, ~16 degrees of latitude, taking 32 N Tucson to ~48-49 N).

**EXACT honest sentence template (use verbatim, fill the bracket with a hedge word):**
> "Climb these 27 miles and the air cools about 25 F, carrying you through the same sequence of vegetation zones you would cross driving [roughly / on the order of] a thousand miles north toward Canada — a similar temperature regime and biome succession, not an identical climate."

Approved softer variant for tighter copy:
> "The summit is about 25 F cooler than the desert floor, which is why the same forest sequence you would drive north for hundreds of miles to reach stacks up in a single mountain."

## 5. Honesty Guardrails

Every overclaim to avoid, drawn verbatim from the research cautions:

- **No spruce, no subalpine.** Do NOT put Engelmann spruce or a true spruce-fir / subalpine zone on Mount Lemmon. The Catalinas top out at Douglas-fir + white fir mixed conifer (~9,157 ft). Only the Pinaleno Mts (Mount Graham, 10,720 ft) have genuine Engelmann spruce-corkbark fir. Travel copy saying "pines, firs and spruce" at the summit is loose; do not repeat it. Note Douglas-fir is *Pseudotsuga* (not a true fir), and white fir is the true fir present; neither is a spruce.
- **"Mexico-to-Canada" is an ANALOGY.** State it as community composition / life-form sequence, never identical climate or species. The summit reaches only Merriam's Canadian zone; the Hudsonian spruce zone and Arctic-Alpine tundra of real Canada lie above the peak and are absent.
- **Never say "the same climate you'd find X miles north."** Climate is more than temperature. Say "a similar temperature regime" or "the same sequence of vegetation zones."
- **Never present a single hard mileage as fact.** Sources disagree by ~2x (Hopkins ~170, Merriam ~200, popular ~300 mi per 1,000 ft). Always hedge with "roughly," "on the order of," or a range.
- **Photoperiod is the killer caveat.** Going up a mountain does not lengthen summer days or shorten winter days the way going north does. A 9,000 ft peak at 32 N keeps Tucson's day-length cycle.
- **Seasonality differs.** Arizona's bimodal winter-storm + summer-monsoon regime is not the moisture/snow pattern of the northern latitude the temperature analogy points to.
- **Match is by physiognomy, not species.** Soils, bedrock, aspect, and fire history differ; the vegetation resemblance is desert-to-conifer form plus temperature, not species-for-species identity.
- **Summit cooling is a spread, not a fixed number.** "20-30 F cooler" is the observed range; "~25 F on average" is defensible; "exactly 30 F cooler" is not.
- **Lapse rate is an average.** Inversions on cold clear mornings can make the valley colder than the slope.
- **Merriam life zones are heritage terminology.** Dated and temperature-only (underweights moisture); fine for outreach/teaching (the Palisades signs use them), flag as heritage, not current classification (modern work uses Brown/Lowe/Pase biotic communities).
- **Bands are historical baselines, not fixed lines.** Boundaries shift with slope aspect and have moved upslope with recent warming (Brusca et al. 2013).
- **Species must stay in their band.** Saguaro is confined to the desert base and does not climb into grassland/oak; ponderosa and Douglas-fir do not descend to the desert.

### Species mix-ups to avoid (Sky Island cross-contamination)
- **No Mount Graham red squirrel** in the Catalinas (endemic to the Pinalenos). The conspicuous tassel-eared squirrel is **Abert's** (introduced); the native tree squirrel is **Arizona gray squirrel**.
- **No Mexican fox squirrel** (Chiricahua only).
- **No Mexican chickadee** (Chiricahua / Animas only). Use **mountain chickadee**.
- **No twin-spotted rattlesnake** (Chiricahua / Pinaleno / Huachuca / Santa Rita only). Use **Sonoran mountain kingsnake** and **black-tailed rattlesnake**; montane lizards are **Yarrow's spiny lizard** and **greater short-horned lizard**.
- **No elk, no Clark's nutcracker, no elegant trogon** as Catalina signatures (trogon is a casual/rare visitor only).
- **Desert tortoise here is the Sonoran species** (*Gopherus morafkai*), not Mojave. High-country horned lizard is greater short-horned, not the desert's regal.

## 6. Build Technique

**Architecture — two-tier progressive-enhancement stack:**
- **Landscape renderer:** layered inline-SVG depth planes. Sky = a CSS-gradient div; ridgelines + vegetation = stacked `<svg>` layers sharing one identical `viewBox`; particles (snow / dust / birds) = a Canvas overlay only, with DPR capped at `Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 2)`. Prefer SVG over Canvas for the static scene (accessible, resolution-independent, zero DPR cost). Budget **~6 moving layers max** (Firewatch uses 6); exaggerate depth with non-linear depth gaps, not more layers.
- **Scroll driver, tier 1 (preferred):** native CSS `animation-timeline: scroll()` for the global climb + `view()` for per-band reveals, gated behind `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` AND `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`.
- **Scroll driver, tier 2 (fallback, mandatory + our baseline):** one `requestAnimationFrame` loop, single reader of `scrollY`, writing ONE `:root` custom property `--progress` (0-1); each layer transforms via `translate3d`.
- **Discrete band triggers:** IntersectionObserver to swap the active biome and fire label fade-ins.
- **Sky morph:** register gradient stop colors as `@property --sky-top` / `--sky-bottom` so they interpolate.
- **Band cross-fade:** each biome = a `position: sticky` full-viewport scene; adjacent bands overlap and cross-fade via opacity.
- **Compositor discipline:** animate only `transform` + `opacity`; `will-change: transform` ONLY on the parallax layers; flat `translateY`, no 3D perspective.
- **Offscreen / hidden pause:** IntersectionObserver pauses the rAF loop + Canvas offscreen; `visibilitychange` pauses on a hidden tab and MUST explicitly resume (rAF freezes on hidden tabs — documented ddl-orrery gotcha).
- **Floors:** `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` renders every band statically in document order. No-JS base layer = full stacked SVG landscape + gradient sky + all biome text. Never scrolljack.

**Reference sites (one trick each):** Firewatch (single-property parallax + reduced-motion off-switch) · NYT Snow Fall (sticky scene-handoff) · Scrollama/The Pudding (IO band triggers).
